- Job process (`src/lib/job.cpp`): each fault is counted and timed from when the network
  thread takes it to when its page is installed or woken, into power-of-two buckets; pages
  sent are counted; once a second while the job runs it queues `L_STATS` for the writer
  thread of M5-1. ponytail: the per-segment summary looks at every page of the region each
  second, fine for the regions tested.
- `src/platform/`: `HttpServer::get_stream()`, a reply streamed until the handler returns,
  with the cpp-httplib adapter's chunked content provider.
- `src/pmd/job_status.{h,cpp}` (new): the JSON of `/v1/jobs` (counts, rates over the last
  second, the histogram with p50 and p99), of `/v1/jobs/{id}/pages`, and the stats event.
  `pmd` keeps each process's last two `L_STATS`; once a second it remakes the JSON under the
  status mutex and adds events: the rates of every job, and joins, leaves, job starts and
  ends found by comparing the peer and job sets with the second before. `/v1/events` streams
  every event from the request on, and returns when the client goes or `pmd` stops.
- docs/STATUS_API.md (new): the five endpoints, each field, an example of each.

  `join`; and `/v1/jobs` is empty again.

## Decisions

<!--
Choices you made inside the card's scope, each with the reason. Not for points listed under
"Still open" in the plan: those are asked, never decided here. Write "None" if there were none.
-->
- **A fault's latency** runs from the network thread taking the fault to its page being
  installed or woken: what the waiting thread sees, less the hop from the fault thread.
- **Sixteen power-of-two buckets in microseconds,** the last open-ended; p50 and p99 are the
  upper bound of the bucket that holds them.
- **Bytes are pages x 4,096;** frame headers are not counted.
- **Joins and leaves are peers of this node's table;** job starts and ends are job processes
  on this node. Both are found once a second by comparing with the second before, so they
  appear within a second of `pmd` knowing.
- **The events kept are the last 600;** a stream further behind skips ahead.
