### 2026-10-07 — session 2: built and checked

- `src/net/transport.h` and its copy in `docs/INTERNAL_API.md`: the approved `net_open` change,
  the same text in both.
- `src/net/epoll_transport.cpp` (about 500 lines). One thread, the caller of `run()`, does all
  socket work and makes every handler call; it waits only in `epoll_wait`. Other threads reach
  it through mutex-guarded queues and an eventfd. No lock is held while a handler runs.
  - Connections: the lower node ID connects and retries every 100 ms until the peer listens;
    the higher accepts, and only from a lower ID it has no connection to. `TCP_NODELAY` on all.
  - Each side sends `JOIN_JOB` first; `on_peer_joined` follows the peer's.
  - Frames are reassembled from the byte stream and checked (header, CRC, job, addressee,
    sender) before delivery; a failure reports the peer lost with `Errc::kProtocol`.
  - A frame's header and payload leave in one `writev`, up to 16 queued frames per call.
  - `HEARTBEAT` every 1 s; 3 s of silence reports the peer lost.
  - A timed request is sent again with `RETRY` after 2 s and reported after 2 s more; only an
    opcode the protocol marks as a reply stops the timer.
- `tests/unit/net_test.cpp`: 6 cases, passed on the first run (12 s; the time is the protocol's
  own timers). clang-tidy found three small things (a widening multiply, a public member and a
  missing `const` in the test), fixed.
- Repeats: 5 runs without a sanitizer, 3 under gcc TSan, 3 under clang ASan: 0 failures.

## Decisions

<!--
Choices you made inside the card's scope, each with the reason. Not for points listed under
"Still open" in the plan: those are asked, never decided here. Write "None" if there were none.
-->
- **Time-based work is checked every 50 ms at most,** or sooner when a caller's timer is due.
  Marked `ponytail:`; a timer heap if it ever costs too much.
- **Only reply opcodes stop a reply timer.** Both ends number their requests from 1, so a
  peer's own request can carry the number of one of ours.
- **A connection attempt that fails is retried quietly;** only a peer that had joined is ever
  reported lost. Start-up order between processes therefore does not matter.
- **The file-descriptor wrapper is repeated from `src/mem/`** (ten lines). Sharing it means a
  header in `src/platform/`, outside this card; marked `ponytail:` for when a third user appears.
- **The transport's own timeouts are constants,** not configuration: the plan lists them as
  fixed by the design.

## Verification

<!--

Local, 2026-10-07 (kernel 7.0.0-38, loopback).

**Done-when, part 1: two processes on localhost exchange 100,000 frames of mixed size with none
lost or reordered.** The test forks a second process that sends every frame straight back.
50,000 frames go out (0 to 2,048 bytes, every 500th one 100,000 bytes) and 50,000 come back;
each echo is compared with what was sent, in order: 0 wrong. The child exits on the first frame
that is missing or out of order; it did not.

**Done-when, part 2: a killed peer is reported within 3 s.** The child is then sent SIGKILL;
`on_peer_lost` arrives well inside 3 s (asserted).

Also: 3.5 s of idle connection is not reported lost (heartbeats), and a peer stopped with
SIGSTOP is, between 1.9 and 4.5 s later; a timed request is seen again with `RETRY` after 2 s
and reported after 4 s, while an answered one is neither; timers fire and can be cancelled; a
joined peer sending a frame with a wrong CRC is reported with `Errc::kProtocol` and the frame is
not delivered; unknown peer and over-long payload are refused.
